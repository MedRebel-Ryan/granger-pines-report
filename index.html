Chapter 02 · The Attribution Gap

The Biggest Leak Is Not the Campaign. It Is the Intake.

One caller voluntarily shared their referral source. Every other phone-in new patient has no traceable origin. The intake question was not asked on a single call across the entire review period. This is not a campaign problem. It is a front desk protocol gap that prevents any channel, postcard or otherwise, from being accurately credited.

How to Fix It

Ask at every call, every time. "How did you hear about us?" takes three seconds and should be a required step before confirming any new patient appointment. Log it in the chart. The answer needs to go into the referral source field in Open Dental or a note in the patient record. If it is not logged, it does not exist for reporting purposes. Capture it after the visit if it was missed on the call. Front desk staff can ask at check-in or check-out.

Staff Perception vs. The Data

Staff have expressed that the November campaign felt more successful. December and November did show stronger new patient volume overall. But that comparison is not a campaign comparison. It is a seasonality comparison. November and December are historically high-volume months for dental new patient acquisition across the industry. May and June are not. Attributing stronger fall performance to the postcard, rather than to seasonal demand, is not supported by any data either campaign produced.
